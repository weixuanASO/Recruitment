## Poh Jing Lin — 3 clips

### Clip 1 — "What did you never think you'd be able to accomplish?"
"So in the past, I was someone who had very low self-confidence and a lot of limiting beliefs. I didn't even dare to declare that I wanted to achieve Million Dollar Round Table in my first few years. However, being under the right mentorship and environment, I managed to actually achieve my goal of Court of the Table in my first few years — and also [moved into] Financial Services Management. So I'm very thankful to this career for showing me that even someone like me, with no sales background and no network, can achieve something I didn't even think I could. I'm immensely grateful for this career for showing me that anything can be possible with heart and hard work."

### Clip 2 — "How did you overcome the struggles?"
"Definitely I felt like I had a lot of self-doubt, and also sleepless nights. I'm really thankful for my team and my mentor for helping me overcome all of these challenges — and how they managed to help me change my perspective towards many things. The mindset is: whenever something happens to us, we don't immediately have a negative thought towards it. And with this mindset, it managed to help me overcome many challenges and see the opportunities that lie within — and it's something that helped me go further in this career. The constant reminder of this during all of my trainings really helped me shape my mindset."

### Clip 3 — "How did you feel about the career initially?"
"Before joining, I actually wanted to work in the maritime sector — that's what I studied at university. Before this, I actually tried another agency first, and took my papers under them. However, I decided to step out of it, because I thought that to do well in financial advisory I needed to be very outspoken and extroverted — and I'm an introverted person, which is why I decided to stop. However, I took another leap of faith to try this career again, and I realised that I do NOT need to be extroverted and outspoken to do well — the mentorship plays a bigger role. In my first three months, passing each paper made me want to press on, [I re-took some] like 3–4 times."

**Jing Lin summary:** University plan was maritime sector. Tried another agency, quit believing she was "too introverted" to succeed. Second attempt: realised mentorship matters more than personality. Started with no sales background or network + low confidence. Under mentorship achieved **Court of the Table** within her first few years (her original private goal was MDRT — a lower tier — which she hadn't dared say aloud). Moved into a **Financial Services Management** role.

## Denzel Chiam — 4 clips

### Clip 4 — "What values connected with you the most?"
"There are a few. One would be being family-oriented. Second would be ethical. But the one that struck me the most was that they emphasise heavily on cross-sharing. Because as a self-employed person in this job, most people would not want to share how they achieved their success. But here, they actually cross-share how they managed to achieve success, so that everybody else benefits from it — which I feel is very important, because ultimately you want to grow together as a team."

### Clip 5 — "What were you doing before?"
"Before this career, I was in NS. I was studying a diploma in Pharmaceutical Sciences — and I really liked what I studied. But when I went to the internship, all I did was use the computer for 8 hours a day, and I like to interact with people more. So what I was looking for in my next career was: I wanted to work hard, but I also wanted to be fairly compensated. Previously I worked a lot of part-time jobs, and they always asked me to OT — but time is money, and I still had to work without getting paid for it. So I wanted to work in an environment where how hard I worked [determined what I earned]."

### Clip 6 — "What did you think the career was like?"
"I initially used to think that I had to do roadshows — because I always see them everywhere, in the MRT or in shopping malls — and I always thought I'd have to hard-sell something to get the sale. But then I remember one time, a very close friend came to me and asked if I'd be down to hear an investment sharing. I was like, huh? I already didn't want to buy anything from her, and I didn't want to be hard-sold. So I said, 'okay lah, I'll go and hear, but I won't buy.' But when she was sharing with me about the things she had, I was like, 'eh, how come you never try to hard-sell anything to me? Why were you genuinely concerned?' — because from my previous experiences with my other financial advisor friends [it was different]. Then I asked her where she was from, and she told me. So I went and searched it up, and one day I came down to the office to see for myself — and I realised the way they work is very different. They don't run roadshows; they work by word of mouth and referral, which I find more appealing. When someone refers you, [there's trust already]."

### Clip 7 — "Were you hesitant? What changed?"
"Initially I was very hesitant to join this industry, because there's a very strong stigma against financial advisors — based on all the past experiences my friends had about being mis-sold, being scammed, or being sold products they didn't need. My other concerns were: is this job sustainable? Will it be replaced by AI? And what will my friends and family think of me? They'd always tell me they don't like financial advisors — and yet I was still going into this line. But surprisingly, after I told them I was joining, they were all very supportive, and they told me: as long as you know what you're doing, and as long as you sell the right thing to the right person, they'll be more than happy — and glad to work with me too."

**Denzel summary:** Ex-NS, Pharmaceutical Sciences diploma. Internship was 8 hrs/day at a computer — wanted people-facing work. Frustrated by unpaid overtime in part-time jobs; wanted effort to equal reward. Preconception: FA = roadshows + hard-selling. Changed his mind after a friend gave him a genuine, no-pressure sharing; visited the office, saw a referral-based (not roadshow) model. Overcame the "stigma" concern and worries about AI / what family thinks — family ended up supportive. **Moved into management in his 2nd year.**
